The Challenge
Saudi Industry Faces Rising Energy Costs
💸
Energy Cost Pressure
Industrial electricity rates have increased significantly. Diesel and LPG remain volatile, exposing manufacturers to unpredictable input costs that erode margins.
🌍
Sustainability Mandates
Vision 2030 environmental targets require Saudi industries to reduce carbon footprint. Regulatory bodies are tightening emission standards for industrial operations.
⚠️
Supply Reliability Risk
Cylinder-based LPG creates operational vulnerability — delivery delays, storage limitations, and quantity restrictions disrupt continuous manufacturing processes.
📉
Competitive Pressure
Regional competitors with access to piped natural gas operate at 30–50% lower fuel costs. Without grid access, Saudi manufacturers face a structural cost disadvantage.

Commercial Framework
Transparent, Regulated Pricing Model
Building Block 1
Return on Capital
WACC-based return on the Regulated Asset Base (RAB). Capital recovery is distributed over asset life — you benefit from declining tariffs as assets depreciate.
Building Block 2–3
Depreciation + Tax
Straight-line depreciation (30-year assets) and Zakat obligations are passed through transparently in the tariff — no hidden margins.
Building Block 4–5
Operating Costs
Controllable OPEX subject to X-factor efficiency mechanism. Pass-through costs (ARAMCO licence, insurance, government fees) recovered at actual cost.
Tariff Mode
Fixed + 5-Year Resets
Tariff fixed for 5-year price control periods, then reset by independent regulator. CPI escalation applies within each period — maximum 2% per annum.
Gas Commodity
Commodity Pass-Through
Gas commodity at SAR 10.31/MMBtu (ARAMCO transfer price). Distribution tariff is separate and stable — commodity price risk can be hedged independently.
